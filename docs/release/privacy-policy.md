Your privacy matters to me. This statement explains what information Markvy handles, where it is kept, and the choices you have.

Markvy is a desktop app for reading and editing Markdown documents on your own computer. It is made and published by Sainath Racherla ("I", "me"). This statement covers the Markvy app on Windows, macOS and Linux, however you installed it, including from the Microsoft Store. It does not cover websites you choose to visit from Markvy, or Microsoft's own services, which have their own privacy statements.

**In short:** Markvy collects no personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting, and it never sends anything to me. Your documents and settings stay on your computer.

---

## Personal data I collect

None. Markvy does not send me your documents, your settings, your name, your email address, your IP address, or any information about how you use it.

To work, Markvy keeps some information **on your own computer only**, in its own app folder. This statement calls it "local data". It is never sent to me or to anyone else.

| Local data | What it is |
|---|---|
| Library | The documents and folders you opened recently, your bookmarks, your open tabs (so they come back next time), and the websites you allowed to show pictures. |
| Preferences | Your reading settings, such as theme, text size, font, line width, and whether editing is turned on. |
| Drafts | While you edit a page, a private copy of your unsaved changes, so a crash or a closed window doesn't lose them. A draft is deleted when you save or discard the changes. |
| Technical log | Short notes about errors: the time, the app version and what went wrong. It never contains the text of your documents. |
| Web view data | Markvy shows pages with the system web view (Microsoft Edge WebView2 on Windows). The web view keeps its own cache in Markvy's app folder. |

Markvy also reads the documents, folders and pictures you choose to open, and changes them only when you press Save, rename or delete. When you paste or insert a picture while editing, Markvy saves it as a file in an `images` folder next to the page. These files belong to you and stay where you keep them.

## How I use personal data

I don't receive any, so I don't use any. Markvy uses its local data only on your computer, to:

- reopen your tabs and recent documents, and show your bookmarks;
- apply your reading preferences;
- recover unsaved changes after a crash;
- show you technical details when something goes wrong, so you can ask for help.

Markvy doesn't build a profile of you, and doesn't use your data for advertising, analytics or training artificial intelligence.

## Reasons I disclose personal data

I don't hold any personal data about Markvy users, so I have none to share, sell or disclose.

Information leaves your computer only when **you** choose to send it:

- **"Copy details."** When an error message offers *Copy details*, Markvy puts the technical details on your clipboard. Nothing is sent. If you paste them into an email to me, you decide what to send.
- **Your documents.** If your documents are in a folder that another service syncs (for example OneDrive, Dropbox or a network drive), that service handles them under its own terms. Markvy doesn't control or contact it.

## How to access and control your personal data

All of Markvy's local data is on your computer, so you control it directly:

- **See or change it.** Recent documents, bookmarks and tabs are in the app. Reading settings are in Preferences. The websites you allowed to show pictures are listed in **Privacy & Help**, where you can remove them.
- **Stop internet pictures.** In Preferences you can block pictures from the internet, or keep Markvy asking each time (the default).
- **Turn off editing.** In Preferences, turn off **Allow editing** and Markvy only reads your files.
- **Delete it.** Uninstall Markvy, or delete its app folder:
  - Windows: `%APPDATA%\com.sainath.markvy` and `%LOCALAPPDATA%\com.sainath.markvy`
  - macOS: `~/Library/Application Support/com.sainath.markvy`
  - Linux: `~/.local/share/com.sainath.markvy`

  If you installed Markvy from the Microsoft Store, uninstalling it removes this data automatically.

Because I hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for me to look up, export or delete. If you have a question about your privacy rights, contact me (see *How to contact me*).

## Cookies and similar technologies

Markvy uses no cookies, web beacons, tracking pixels or advertising identifiers. When it fetches a picture you allowed, it sends no cookies with the request.

## Accounts

Markvy has no accounts and no sign-in. You never need to give me your name, email address or any other details to use it.

## Collection of data from children

Markvy collects no personal data from anyone, including children. It has no chat, no sharing between users, no purchases and no account to create.

## Storage and processing of personal data

All local data is stored on your own computer, in Markvy's app folder. Nothing is stored on my servers (I don't run any for Markvy), and I transfer nothing to another country.

## Advertising

Markvy shows no advertising and shares no data with advertisers.

## Security of personal data

Markvy is designed to keep what it does on your computer small and contained:

- The part of Markvy that shows pages has no direct access to your files. It can reach files only through a fixed set of commands built into Markvy.
- Markvy reads only files inside a folder you opened, including pictures, and refuses shortcuts that point outside it.
- Documents are cleaned before they are shown, and a strict content security policy applies. Diagrams and formulas run in their libraries' restricted modes.
- Pictures from the internet are fetched only after you allow it, and only pictures of up to 5 MB are accepted.

**Limitation.** The questions before opening a website or showing a picture from the internet are asked by the part of Markvy that shows pages. If a flaw ever let a document get past the cleaning and the content security policy, it could make Markvy contact a website, or open one in your browser, without asking. No independent security review of these protections has been done yet.

Your local data is protected by your computer's own security, such as your user account, password and disk encryption. Anyone who can use your computer account can read Markvy's app folder.

## Where I store and process personal data

Only on your computer. See *Storage and processing of personal data*.

## My retention of personal data

I retain nothing. On your computer:

- drafts are deleted when you save or discard your changes;
- the technical log is limited in size, and older entries are replaced by newer ones;
- the library and preferences are kept until you change them, delete the app folder or uninstall Markvy.

## When Markvy connects to the internet

Markvy works offline. It never contacts me, never checks for updates, and never sends usage or crash information. It connects to the internet only in these cases:

- **Pictures from the internet.** If a document contains a picture stored on a website, Markvy asks before showing it. If you agree (once, or always for that website), Markvy downloads that picture from that website. As with any web request, the website can see your computer's IP address and that the picture was requested. Markvy sends no cookies, no account details and nothing from your documents.
- **Web and email links.** When you open a link, Markvy passes it to your web browser or email program. Markvy asks first unless you turned that question off in Preferences. What happens next is governed by that program and that website.

The websites you choose to contact have their own privacy policies. I don't control them.

## Microsoft Store and Windows

If you install Markvy from the Microsoft Store, Microsoft handles the download, licensing and updates, under the Microsoft Privacy Statement. As the publisher, I receive the summary reports Microsoft gives every Store publisher, such as install counts, ratings and reviews, and crash counts by Windows version. These reports don't identify you, and Markvy itself sends nothing to Microsoft.

On Windows, Markvy displays pages with Microsoft Edge WebView2, which is part of Windows. Microsoft may collect diagnostic data from WebView2 according to your Windows diagnostic data settings and the Microsoft Privacy Statement. You can change those settings in Windows Settings › Privacy & security › Diagnostics & feedback.
